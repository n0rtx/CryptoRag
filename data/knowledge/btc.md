# Bitcoin (BTC) — overview

Bitcoin (BTC) is the first cryptocurrency, described in the white paper "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System" published in 2008 by the pseudonymous Satoshi Nakamoto. The network launched on 3 January 2009 with the genesis block. Биткоин (BTC) — первая криптовалюта, запущенная 3 января 2009 года; автор — аноним под псевдонимом Сатоши Накамото.

## Consensus and technology

Bitcoin uses Proof of Work (PoW) with the SHA-256 hashing algorithm. Miners compete to find a valid block hash; the winner adds a block and receives the block subsidy plus transaction fees. The target block time is about 10 minutes, and difficulty adjusts roughly every 2016 blocks (about two weeks). Консенсус — Proof of Work (SHA-256), блок примерно каждые 10 минут, сложность пересчитывается каждые 2016 блоков.

Bitcoin uses the UTXO (unspent transaction output) model. Its scripting language is intentionally limited and not Turing complete. The base layer processes only a few transactions per second, so scaling is done through second-layer solutions such as the Lightning Network (payment channels). Segregated Witness (SegWit, 2017) and Taproot (2021) are major protocol upgrades.

## Supply and halving

Maximum supply is hard-capped at 21 million BTC. New coins are issued as block rewards, and the reward is cut in half roughly every 210,000 blocks (about four years) — the halving. Halvings occurred in 2012 (50 → 25 BTC), 2016 (25 → 12.5), 2020 (12.5 → 6.25) and April 2024 (6.25 → 3.125). The last bitcoin is expected to be mined around the year 2140. Максимальная эмиссия — 21 млн BTC; халвинг происходит примерно раз в 4 года и уменьшает награду за блок вдвое.

## Use cases and risks

Main narratives: "digital gold" / store of value, censorship-resistant settlement, and a hedge against inflation. Spot Bitcoin ETFs were approved in the United States in January 2024, increasing institutional access. Risks: high price volatility, regulatory changes, custody and key-loss risk, energy-consumption criticism of PoW, and the long-term question of security funding once block subsidies decline. Bitcoin's fees are paid in BTC; the smallest unit is a satoshi (1 BTC = 100,000,000 sats).
